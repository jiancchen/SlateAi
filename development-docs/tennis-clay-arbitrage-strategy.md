# Tennis Clay Arbitrage Strategy

## Core Idea

This is not a pure `pick the winner` strategy.

The real target is:
- prediction-market contracts where the favorite is priced so high that the underdog only needs to stay live early
- clay-court matches where holding serve and dragging the set to `4-4`, `5-5`, or a tiebreak can re-rate the dog contract from `9-15c` into `28-40c`
- situations where the dog does **not** need to win the match for the trade to work

The trade is the edge.
The outright winner is secondary.

## Why Clay Is Different

Clay changes the shape of the trade:
- favorites do not separate as cleanly as they do on grass or quick hard courts
- breaks matter more, but they also take more work
- if the underdog can hold through the first couple service turns, the market often overreacts in the dog’s favor
- a favorite who is “supposed to roll” can look expensive if the set simply stays on serve

This is a clay arbitrage framework.
Do not blindly port it to grass or faster hard courts.

## The Best Contract Shape

The cleanest setup is:
- favorite priced `85-99c`
- dog priced `1-15c`
- dog has at least one believable path to:
  - hold the first two service games
  - keep the set on serve through `4-4`
  - push the favorite into real break points
  - or steal one early break and force a market repricing

That is where the biggest reaction bands live.

If both players are:
- `50-50`
- `55-45`
- `60-40`

then the arbitrage is much smaller.
There is still trading value, but not the same `3x` or `4x` contract compression.

## The Screen

Before entering a dog contract on clay, check:

1. Is the favorite truly dominant on clay this year?
- not just reputation
- not just ranking
- actual clay form
- actual recent openings
- actual ability to break quickly

2. Has the favorite shown early weakness on clay?
- slow starts
- service wobbles
- weak conversion in the first set
- dropped sets to live underdogs

3. Has the dog shown clay resistance?
- held serve against stronger opponents
- pushed sets deep
- played tiebreaks or `7-5 / 7-6` type clay sets
- forced break points even in losses

4. Is the matchup likely to stay on serve long enough?
- big server
- heavy first strike
- favorite not an elite return machine on clay
- or favorite more comfortable winning slowly than breaking immediately

5. Is the dog’s price absurdly low relative to that path?
- `9c` dog who can plausibly reach `4-4` is tradable
- `33c` dog in a near-even match is not the same edge

## Entry Logic

Best entry styles:
- pre-match when the dog is badly underpriced
- after the first clean hold if the favorite still looks flat
- after the dog survives one or two pressure service games

What matters:
- the set is still alive
- the favorite has not already opened daylight
- the market still treats the dog like a dead longshot

## Exit Logic

Primary exits:
- `4-4` on serve
- `5-5` on serve
- tiebreak entry
- dog earns the first break
- favorite shows visible frustration or service instability

Break points are the real trigger.
If the dog reaches:
- `4-4`
- `5-4*` pressure spots
- `30-30` / `deuce` on a favorite serve

the contract can jump enough to make the trade worth it without requiring the upset.

## Red Flags

Do **not** overtrade:
- even-money or near-even markets
- favorites who are actually crushing clay openers
- dogs who cannot hold serve at all
- matchups where the dog’s only path is a miracle, not a real service-hold pattern
- grass-style reads on clay

## What Counts As Success

Success is not:
- “the dog won”

Success is:
- “the dog stayed alive long enough for the market to re-rate”
- “the contract moved from `9-15c` into a `25-40c` sell zone”
- “the first set created pressure without requiring a full upset”

## Good Example Type

The ideal example is exactly this:
- huge favorite
- big clay name
- not perfect on clay this year
- underdog has shown enough serve or resistance to keep the first set close
- dog contract is priced like zero
- you sell after `4-4`, `5-5`, a tiebreak, or the first real break-pressure swing

That is the clay-market arbitrage lane we want to keep building around.
