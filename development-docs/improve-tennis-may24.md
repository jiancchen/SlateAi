# Improve Tennis After May 24, 2026

## Snapshot
- Final graded tennis record for the May 24 Roland Garros Day 1 desk: `25-11`
- ATP record: `14-6`
- WTA record: `11-5`
- Pending carryover from the original board: `Jannik Sinner vs Clement Tabur` on May 25

The broad result was good, but it was not good in one uniform way. The ATP side carried the board. The women’s side was still much more volatile, especially when the desk tried to get clever in toss-up or “live dog” spots without a cleaner matchup edge.

## What Actually Worked

### 1. ATP clay structure was still the cleanest lane
The strongest men’s calls mostly held when the desk stayed close to:
- better clay baseline quality
- cleaner five-set problem solving
- more stable serve-plus-return shape
- simpler favorite path

Examples:
- `Karen Khachanov`
- `Alexander Zverev`
- `Novak Djokovic`
- `Joao Fonseca`
- `Jakub Mensik`
- `Hamad Medjedovic`

### 2. A few anti-name WTA reads were genuinely good
The desk was not just good when it followed the bigger name. It also hit when the current-shape argument was specific and real.

Best examples:
- `Hailey Baptiste over Barbora Krejcikova`
- `Francesca Jones over Beatriz Haddad Maia`
- `Solana Sierra over Emma Raducanu`
- `Sara Bejlek over Sloane Stephens`

### 3. “Current trust” beat résumé in several spots
That was the right instinct. The problem was not the idea. The problem was applying it too broadly.

## What Failed

### 1. Too many WTA live-dog ideas were still too loose
The misses were not random. A lot of them came from trying to make a story out of volatility without enough real structural support.

Examples:
- `Clara Burel` lost to `Katie Volynets`
- `Tereza Valentova` lost to `Magda Linette`
- `Sara Sorribes Tormo` lost to `Tamara Korpatsch`
- `Clara Tauson` lost to `Daria Snigur`

These were not terrible ideas in a vacuum, but they were not strong enough to deserve full prediction status.

### 2. The desk still overreached in some coin-flip ATP spots
The ATP board was better than the WTA board, but it still leaked when the matchup was actually thin.

Examples:
- `Tomas Martin Etcheverry` lost to `Nuno Borges`
- `Hugo Dellien` lost to `Valentin Royer`
- `Gabriel Diallo` lost to `James Duckworth`

The lesson is the same: not every “reasonable lean” should become a real board pick.

### 3. Confidence still needs more separation
Too many matches lived in the `58-68` confidence zone. That makes the board feel busier than it should.

That range needs to split into:
- `real play`
- `watch only`
- `pass`

## What To Change Immediately

### 1. Separate ATP and WTA decision rules
Do not treat them as one product.

Use:
- more trust for ATP clay structure
- more restraint on WTA unless the edge is clear

### 2. Add a harder `pass` gate
A match should not become an official pick unless it has at least one clean reason:
- strong surface edge
- clear return/serve mismatch
- better long-match stability
- clear current-form edge against real competition

If the match is just “kind of live,” it should be `watch`, not `pick`.

### 3. Add opponent-quality weighting to recent form
“Current form” is too weak by itself.

Need:
- last 8-10 clay matches
- weighted by opponent quality
- with separate flags for:
  - soft schedule boost
  - real top-level clay wins
  - retirement / injury distortion

### 4. Add a `closeout / long-match` layer for ATP
The men’s board did well when the favorite had the better five-set problem-solving path. That should become an explicit feature instead of only living in manual writing.

Need:
- best-of-five hold under pressure
- tiebreak and deciding-set stability
- comeback / closeout profile

### 5. Lower trust on WTA volatility dogs by default
For WTA, the model should require more than:
- crowd narrative
- live dog aura
- name-brand skepticism

It should need a stronger technical reason:
- return pressure edge
- clay movement advantage
- opponent injury or rust
- recent match quality edge

## What To Change In The Site Copy

The tennis board should stop sounding like:
- “live dog”
- “interesting opener”
- “crowd might be too hot”

unless that maps to something specific.

Better language:
- `clean favorite`
- `surface edge`
- `return-pressure edge`
- `fitness question`
- `five-set edge`
- `coin flip`
- `pass`

## Practical Rule For May 25 And Forward

1. Promote clean ATP clay favorites more aggressively.
2. Demote WTA toss-ups unless there is a real matchup advantage.
3. Do not force a pick in every medium-confidence match.
4. Treat the `58-68` band as the main danger zone.
5. Let the board be smaller if that is what the slate deserves.

## Bottom Line

May 24 was a good tennis day, but it confirmed that the current board is best when it is:
- simple
- surface-aware
- ATP-first
- skeptical of loose WTA stories

The next version should not try to be smarter by making more calls.
It should try to be sharper by making fewer, cleaner ones.
