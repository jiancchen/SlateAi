- Record: 23-8

## What Worked

- ATP best-of-five structure was the cleanest part of the card. Ruud, Rublev, Djokovic, Paul, Fonseca, Zverev, Khachanov, Mensik, Michelsen, Jodar, De Jong, and Carreno Busta landed.
- The better joins into recent clay opponent quality and Flashscore service rows made the detail pages materially stronger than the earlier ranking-only pass.
- Source-site calls were demoted to context instead of being treated as the desk pick.

## What Missed

- WTA volatility was still under-discounted. Rybakina over Starodubtseva and Paolini over Sierra were high-profile misses where ranking and name strength hid upset risk.
- Several misses were not pure side errors; they were price errors. A favorite can be likely and still not worth buying if the payout is tiny.
- The board did not elevate O/U and game-spread logic enough, even though many matches were close, long, or break-heavy.

## May 28 Adjustments

- High probability is not the same as bet confidence. Favorites need price room before they are tagged as actionable.
- ATP can carry stronger confidence than WTA when rank, clay form, opponent quality, and service profile align.
- WTA favorites get a volatility haircut unless hold rate, return pressure, and recent opponent strength all agree.
- O/U leans should start from recent hold, first-serve won, ace rate, break frequency, and best-of-five extension risk.
