## What Went Right

### The MLB side board was directionally useful
`5-2` full game is a good baseball outcome on a short slate, especially compared with the uglier May 18 profile. The board was right on:
- `Pirates`
- `Braves`
- `Blue Jays`
- `Athletics`
- `Diamondbacks`

### The tennis main-tour board was solid again
The clay-point and form read held reasonably well on the ATP side and was positive overall at `7-5`, even though the WTA quarterfinals stayed more fragile than the ATP quarterfinals.

### The Athletics call deserves credit, but with context
The board got `Athletics over Angels` right, but this was not a clean control read:
- Athletics trailed `2-0` after five
- they came back late and won `3-2`

So it was a correct side, but more of a `late resilience / opponent fade` win than an easy pregame hammer.

## What Went Wrong

### Not all wins were equally good
The danger in a `5-2` baseball day is pretending every winner was clean. That would be false.

Examples:
- `Athletics` won, but only after trailing `2-0` after five.
- `Diamondbacks` won `2-1`, but the game was `0-0` after five, so the early edge was not really there.
- `Mets @ Nationals` was a clean miss: the board took `Nationals`, but `Mets` won `2-1`.

That means May 21 was a good `winner` day, but not a perfect `read quality` day.

## What This Says About The Model

### Stronger areas on May 21
- MLB side selection
- ATP clay form and point-profile reads
- distinguishing some fake baseball favorite traps from real control spots

### Weak areas on May 21
- HR concentration
- WTA weekly-form weighting
- separating `won` from `won cleanly`

## Practical Takeaway

May 21 was a real improvement from the worst baseball days, but it still should not be over-celebrated:
- MLB sides: good
- Tennis main tour: useful
- HR board: still weak

The right grading is:
- a `good` baseball side day
- a `solid` tennis day
- a `bad` HR day

And the archive should keep that separation visible instead of flattening everything into one single “day grade.”
